Training guide · deep core

Stomach vacuum, from first breath to advanced

You breathe out fully and draw your belly in towards your spine without breathing in again. The exercise trains the transversus abdominis, the deep abdominal muscle that works like a built-in weight belt.

5 levels No equipment 3–5 min per session Train on an empty stomach

Before you start

What you get

  • A stronger deep core and better body control.
  • Better posture and a steadier trunk in other exercises.
  • Better control over the trunk. The exercise strengthens the muscle but does not by itself give a smaller waist.
  • It does not burn belly fat. Fat loss comes from diet and overall activity.

Wait or ask a doctor if you

  • are pregnant or have recently given birth (diastasis recti)
  • have a hernia, reflux, high blood pressure, heart or vascular disease or glaucoma
  • have a back injury, a slipped disc or joint problems in wrists, knees or shoulders
  • have had abdominal surgery recently
  • get dizziness, headache, chest pain or shortness of breath during the exercise. Stop at once, and seek care for chest pain or shortness of breath.

Five ground rules

  • Empty stomach. Train in the morning or at least 2–3 hours after a meal.
  • Breathe out first. Breathe out calmly and fully, without forcing, before you draw the belly in. That engages the deep abdominal muscle.
  • Keep the chest still. Your ribs should not lift. The abdominal muscle does the work, not the chest.
  • Frequency. Daily at levels 1–2, every other day at levels 3–4 and 2–3 sessions a week at level 5.
  • Dizziness. If you feel dizzy, light-headed or get a headache: stop and breathe normally.

Belly rises as you breathe in

Level 1 · Beginner

Belly breathing

2 minPer session
DailyFrequency
LyingPosition
  1. Lie on your back with bent knees and feet on the floor. Put one hand on your chest and the other on your belly.
  2. Breathe in through your nose for 4 seconds. The hand on your belly should rise, the hand on your chest should stay still.
  3. Breathe out slowly through your mouth for 6 seconds. Feel the belly sink towards the floor.
  4. Repeat for 10–15 breaths.

Done when: you can move the breath to your belly without your chest moving.

Belly in towards the spine as you breathe out

Level 2 · Beginner

Lying vacuum

10–20 sHold
4–5Sets
30 sRest
  1. Lie on your back with bent knees. Breathe in calmly through your nose.
  2. Breathe out calmly through your mouth until your lungs feel empty. Don't force it.
  3. Without breathing in: draw your navel towards your spine as far as it goes.
  4. Hold the position. Then breathe in and relax.

Tip: Imagine buttoning a pair of trousers that are too tight. Let your arms rest along your sides. If you feel dizzy or get a headache: stop and breathe normally.

Done when: you hold for 20 seconds with even tension and no shaking.

Gravity helps you

Level 3 · Intermediate

Vacuum on all fours

20–30 sHold
3–5Sets
30 sRest
  1. Get on all fours: hands under your shoulders, knees under your hips, back straight.
  2. Let your belly hang towards the floor and breathe in.
  3. Breathe out fully and draw your navel towards your spine and upwards.
  4. Hold the position and keep a neutral spine. Relax and breathe in.

This is often the easiest position for feeling the muscle work, because the belly hangs down and you can see exactly how far it moves.

Done when: you hold for 30 seconds and can talk calmly in short sentences while holding.

Standing and seated

Level 4 · Intermediate

Standing and seated vacuum

30–45 sHold
3–5Sets
20 sRest
  1. Standing with support: lean your torso slightly forward with your hands on your thighs and knees slightly bent.
  2. Breathe out fully and draw your belly in. Hold.
  3. Upright: when that feels steady, do the same with straight legs and your hands on your hips.
  4. Seated: do the same on a chair with a straight back, so you can train anywhere.

Keep your shoulders down and your jaw relaxed. Don't tense your glutes or thighs, only your belly. If you feel dizzy: sit down, stop and breathe normally.

Done when: you hold for 45 seconds standing without support.

Vacuum under load

Level 5 · Advanced

Longer holds and movement

4–6Pulses per set
3–5Sets
20 sRest
  1. Pulses: hold the vacuum for 10 seconds, release, take 2–3 calm breaths and draw in again. Do 4–6 pulses per set.
  2. Plank with vacuum: get into a plank on your knees or toes. Breathe out, draw your belly in and hold for 10–20 seconds.
  3. Vacuum in motion: walk slowly with your belly drawn in and breathe calmly. In a squat: breathe out and draw in as you stand up, breathe in before the next rep. Never hold your breath under load.
  4. After each set: breathe in calmly and rest. Stop at once if you feel dizzy.

The yoga variation Nauli (rolling the muscles) is a separate technique. Learn it only once you master the vacuum in every position. Skip Nauli with a hernia, pregnancy, high blood pressure, stomach ulcers or recent abdominal surgery.

Maintenance: 2–3 sessions a week is enough to keep your level.

Common mistakes

You don't breathe out fully

With air left in your lungs the belly won't draw in. Breathe out calmly until you can't any more, without forcing it.

Your chest lifts

Then you are cheating with your ribs. Keep your ribcage down and let your shoulders drop.

You train right after eating

It can feel unpleasant and is harder to control. Wait 2–3 hours.

You skip levels

Standing vacuum without the lying basics is harder to control and often tenses the wrong muscles.
